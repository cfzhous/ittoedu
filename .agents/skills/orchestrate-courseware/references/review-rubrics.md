# Human review rubrics

## Contents

1. Teaching design
2. Content specification
3. Presentation script
4. Visual direction
5. Implementation handoff

These rubrics guide review; they do not auto-approve artifacts.

## 1. Teaching design

Confirm:

- objectives are observable and appropriate for the audience;
- every objective has meaningful evidence, not mere completion or clicking;
- each teaching stage contributes to an objective, misconception repair, or transfer;
- prerequisites and content boundary are explicit;
- strategy and sequence are plausible for the use context;
- the duration reflects teaching and learner thinking time.

Reject a design that uses activity names in place of content, or adds generic quizzes/summaries without evidence value.

## 2. Content specification

Confirm:

- every learner-facing task is complete without hidden chat context;
- facts, givens, answers, reasoning, units, notation, and distractors are correct;
- alternative valid responses and invalid boundaries are defined;
- difficulty is justified by cognitive demand, not topic label or visual complexity;
- the set has enough depth, variation, reflection, and transfer for the planned duration;
- answer reveal order protects productive thinking;
- authoritative or reviewed-original sources are recorded.

For a 45-minute lesson, require a minute budget that includes reading, independent thinking, discussion/checkpoint, feedback, repair, and consolidation. Seven pages do not imply 45 minutes.

## 3. Presentation script

Confirm:

- the initial view gives learners the information required for the first action;
- the learner always knows what to do and why;
- action and response form a visible causal relationship;
- errors receive diagnosis and a recoverable next step;
- classification, sorting, dragging, or clicking has instructional value;
- teacher checkpoints and learner independence match the approved design;
- the final summary is earned from prior evidence;
- every stable HTML frame and static export remains meaningful.

Read the script as if no implementation and no chat existed. If a cold-start implementer must invent essential content or behavior, reject it.

## 4. Visual direction

Confirm:

- visual hierarchy, primary representation, and interaction target are immediately clear;
- each beat may use a composition suited to its task while retaining a coherent design language;
- decorative motion does not obscure instructional causality;
- typography, mathematical/scientific notation, contrast, focus, and reduced motion are addressed;
- assets exist or have an approved generation/licensing route;
- HTML and PDF/PPTX expectations are explicit.

Reject automatic slide chrome, repetitive card grids, or template styling that weakens the approved experience.

## 5. Implementation handoff

Confirm:

- every upstream artifact is the exact approved hash;
- all blocking decisions are resolved;
- authoritative content and editable text boundaries are named;
- delivery formats and expected static differences are explicit;
- acceptance evidence can prove the approved experience;
- change-control return points are clear.

Approval here means implementation may begin. It does not mean the final outcome is accepted.
